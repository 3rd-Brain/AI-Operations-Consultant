# Rollout and Adoption

Used by the rollout specialist (`prompts/rollout-plan.md`). It collects what practitioners do to get people working a new way, with sources. The plan picks what fits the client's team; nothing here is required.

## Why it gets its own plan

- Adoption often takes longer than the build. OpenAI's working system at Morgan Stanley took 6 to 8 weeks to build, then about four more months of pilots and trust-building before advisers adopted it ([Colin Jarvis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cBD7_R-Cizg&t=226s)).
- Salesforce runs adoption and change management, and go-live support, as named phases of every forward-deployed engagement ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/salesforce-launches-forward-deployed-engineer-partner-network-announcement/)).
- Michael Hammer later said reengineering underweighted the human side; 50 to 70 percent of 1990s reengineering efforts missed their goals ([retold by Varick](https://www.varickagents.com/blog/don-t-apply-ai)).
- The skill's own canon: jumping to tools before people and process leaves shelf-ware, and most of the move from Level 2 to Level 3 is education and adoption, not technical integration (`SKILL.md`, Framework canon).

## Training formats a small team will use

From the Digital Operations Playbook workbook (`references/playbook/workbook.md`, Step 2: Education & Training Strategy):

- **Recorded training:** short on-demand videos anyone can rewatch.
- **Workshops:** live sessions on specific use cases.
- **One-on-one sessions:** for people who need more focused help.
- **Office hours:** a standing time for questions and troubleshooting.
- **A one-page guide** for the new steps, kept next to the work.

## Encouraging use

- **Leadership buy-in:** the leadership team is aligned and champions the change within their teams (workbook, Step 3).
- **The owner's mandate:** using the training and the new way is not optional, and time is blocked for learning (workbook, Steps 2 and 4).
- **Start with a pilot group** that tests it on real work before everyone switches (workbook, Step 3).
- **Incentives tied to using it** (workbook, Step 3).
- **A champion on the team** people can ask first.
- **Recognition or a friendly competition** for using the new way (Michael, 2026-10-08).
- **A help bot** that answers "how do I…" inside the team's own tools (Michael, 2026-10-08). Building one is its own Scope.
- **Operators have to want the system** ([Varick](https://www.varickagents.com/blog/don-t-apply-ai)): involve the people whose work changes in the design, not only the rollout.

## Going live

- Use as few steps as the risk allows. Often that is switching on after the test run (Michael, 2026-10-08).
- Oversight follows risk and reversibility, set in the workflow's Step sort: the step acts alone, acts and tells someone, or asks for approval (Michael, 2026-10-09).
- No trial where staff check every output first (shadow mode) by default: only when the Scope's Eval plan cannot prove its bars before launch, or the client asks. Then staff approve or edit what it produces until it beats the average employee ([Vas Moza](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14n5YbMJ3bo&t=283s)), and the Eval plan sets the bar for dropping the checks.
- Run the manual process in parallel during testing (Digital Operations Playbook, AI implementation appendix).

## Checking use

- Report the share of work done by hand, with help, or fully automated, instead of a vague "adoption" figure ([Varick](https://www.varickagents.com/blog/ai-adoption-is-a-myth)).
- Read it from the system's own records first. Monitoring people while they work, such as screen recording or usage tracking, is an option when the client agrees, though it is often hard to run (Michael, 2026-10-08). The UK regulator notes that consent rarely works between employer and employee, and treats keystroke logging and screenshots as high-risk monitoring ([UK ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/employment/monitoring-workers/data-protection-and-monitoring-workers/)).

## Handing over

- A named business owner takes ownership of the new way after launch, with a sign-off ([UK benefits management guide](https://apm.org.uk/media/5csnk4oe/guideforeffectivebenefitsmanagementinmajorprojects.pdf#page=40)). In the method that is the Project owner.
